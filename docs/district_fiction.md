# Seven answers to one question

*Companion to [district_geometry_plan.md](district_geometry_plan.md). That
document says what to build. This one says what it is.*

---

## The question

The facility does not stay where you left it. It stays where you **looked**.

Everything else follows. A corridor holds its shape as long as a witness is in
it; a threshold you have anchored holds after you go. Everything unattended is
provisional — not moving, exactly, but *not committed*, the way a word you
haven't decided to say yet is not yet a word.

Nobody knows whether the facility was built this way or became this way. What is
certain is that its occupants knew, and that every district is somebody's answer
to the same question:

> *How do you make a building stay put when it only stays put while you are
> looking at it?*

Seven answers. All of them worked, for a while. None of them worked.

---

## The Welcome — *Facet Monument*

**The answer: build for a witness so important the building would never dare
change in front of them.**

You come up out of somewhere lower and the corridor stops being a corridor. The
walls step back from you as they rise, three times, each step deeper than the
last, and every reveal has a line of light in it running away to a vanishing
point you cannot find. There are no lamps. The light is *in the joint*. Whoever
built this understood that a lamp is an object and an object has a size, and
they did not want you to know what size anything was.

The axis runs eleven cells without deviating and terminates in a raised dais
with nothing on it. Not "nothing left on it" — nothing was ever on it. The dais
is the point. It is a place to be seen standing.

The Welcome is the most stable district in the facility and the most useless.
Nothing here connects to anywhere you need to go, because it was never built to
be *travelled*. It was built to be **arrived at**, by someone, once. They put
their whole understanding of permanence into a room for a guest.

The guest did not come. The building has been holding its breath ever since,
and it holds perfectly.

> *What a team learns: this is the safest ground in the facility and it will not
> take you anywhere. Cross it, don't live in it.*

## The Noon — *Overlit Grid*

**The answer: if there is nothing to observe, there is nothing to lose.**

It is always noon here. Not bright — *even*. The ceilings float free of the
walls with a slot of light all round, so the light arrives from every direction
at once and nothing casts a shadow, including you. Look at your own feet: no
shadow. The floor meets the wall in a cove you cannot quite locate the corner
of.

The Noon's builders reasoned it out honestly and were, technically, correct.
Decoherence takes hold of the *distinguishable*. A district with no landmark, no
shadow, no scale cue and no corner offers the process nothing to grab. So they
sanded the whole quarter smooth. No feature, nothing to fix, nothing to lose.

They were right. Nothing here changes. It is also true that nobody who walks
into the Noon can tell you, afterward, how long they were in it or which way
they came in, and the ones who go a long way in describe the same thing: a
growing certainty that they had already been down this corridor, followed
immediately by the certainty that they had not, and no way at all to settle it.

The Noon does not decohere. **You** do.

> *What a team learns: your map is worthless here and so is your memory. Anchor
> early or come out where you went in.*

## The Back — *Liminal Grid*

**The answer: none. Nobody answered. Nobody was ever supposed to be here.**

The ceiling is close over your head — panel, panel, panel, and a light in every
third — and it goes on past where you can see in every direction, and there are
columns in it standing on a perfect grid holding up nothing that needs holding.
The openings between rooms have no doors and no frames. The wall just stops.

This is service space. Back-of-house. The volume between the rooms that mattered,
where the building kept its runs and its plant and its slack, and nobody ever
drew it because nobody was ever meant to look at it.

Then the facility started rebuilding itself, and it rebuilt what it had most of.

The Back is now the largest district by volume. It is still growing. It grows
because it is uniform enough to copy and unloved enough that no witness ever
stands in it long enough to pin it down, and every hour it is not observed it
produces a little more of itself. Not a maze — a maze implies a designer. It's a
*tiling*. There is no centre and no edge and no wrong turn, because there is no
turn that is any more right.

Teams that get lost in the Back do not report fear. They report **embarrassment**
— the specific, awful feeling of having lost something in a room with nothing in
it.

> *What a team learns: this district is not trying to kill you. It is not trying
> to do anything. That is the problem.*

## The Well — *Wellshaft*

**The answer: never stop looking, in shifts, forever.**

One shaft. Sixteen storeys of it, a walkway ring at every level with a rail low
enough to lean on, and a stair that does not go beside the void but *around* it,
so that every step down is also a step around and you are never not looking into
the middle.

The Well's answer was social, and it is the only answer here that a person could
love. They didn't try to out-think the process. They just arranged themselves so
that the shaft was never unobserved — everybody's front door opened onto the
same drop, and the drop was full of neighbours, at every hour, at every level.
You held the building up by living in it where everyone could see you.

It worked for as long as there were enough of them.

You can still read the arithmetic in the geometry: the landings that cantilever
out over the void are the good addresses, the ones with the widest view of the
most levels. The grating underfoot is so you could see down through your own
floor. Every design decision in this district is about **sightlines between
people**, which is a strange and moving thing to find in a building.

Now the walkways are empty and the shaft holds anyway, out of habit, the way a
room stays warm for a while.

> *What a team learns: you can see six levels at once here — and so can everyone
> else. Nothing you do in the Well is private.*

## The Index — *Infinite Gallery*

**The answer: write it down.**

Hexagonal galleries, indefinite in number, each with an air shaft through the
middle and a rail around the shaft so low it is more of an insult than a
precaution. Four walls of shelves, five shelves to a wall, twenty to a room.
Two faces open onto hallways. In each hallway a closet the size of a standing
body, and a spiral stair going up and down out of sight.

Somebody decided the facility could be *fixed by being recorded*. Not
photographed — surveyed. Every cell, every threshold, every connection, written
out longhand, because a described room is an observed room, and an observed room
holds.

They were not wrong about the principle. They were wrong about the size of the
job. A complete description of a changing structure has to be at least as large
as the structure, and it has to be rewritten whenever the structure moves, and
the surveyors needed somewhere to keep it, so they built somewhere, and the
somewhere needed surveying.

The Index is now bigger than the thing it indexes. The shelves are full. The
volumes describe rooms that no longer exist, in a district built to hold
descriptions of rooms, and somewhere in it — this is not a metaphor, the
surveyors' own notes are explicit about it — there is a shelf holding an
accurate description of that shelf.

> *What a team learns: everything here is labelled and none of the labels are
> current. It is the best map in the facility and it will walk you into a wall.*

## The Thin — *Thinning*

**The answer: own so little that there is nothing to take.**

A floor. Posts. A roof, and under the roof a rhythm of rafters you can count.
Between the posts, nothing — or a screen, if someone left one, and mostly they
did not. The floor steps up at the threshold, a hand's height, so that entering
is something your body does rather than something you walk through. Around the
outside runs a raised deck under a deep eave: not inside, not outside, a place
to stop.

The Thin's builders came last, and they had watched all the others fail. The
Welcome held a room for a guest who never came. The Noon erased itself. The
Index buried itself. The Back was never anything. The Well needed people and ran
out.

So they subtracted. They asked what the smallest structure was that a person
could stand in and call a room, and they built only that, and they left the
walls out on the grounds that a wall is mostly a thing that can be moved.

It holds. It holds better than the Welcome, and the Welcome is a monument. There
is almost nothing here for the process to get hold of, and what there is, a
person can see all of from one position — which turns out to be the entire
trick, arrived at by giving up rather than by trying harder.

The Thin is empty in the way a made bed is empty. Somebody finished here.

> *What a team learns: sightlines are perfect and cover is nonexistent. You will
> see them coming and they will see you.*

## The Unwitnessed — *Megastructure*

**The answer: there is no answer. This is what happens without one.**

There is no district here. There is only what the facility does when nobody has
looked at it for a very long time.

Shafts that do not agree — one runs eighteen storeys, the one beside it eleven,
the one beside that starts nine levels up and has no bottom you can find. A
walkway ends in a raw edge over a drop, not broken, just *finished*, the way a
sentence stops when the speaker loses interest. Storeys are missing from the
middle of stacks. Conduits four metres thick cross a room from one wall to the
other, and there is nothing at either end of them; they enter and they leave.

Nothing here is human-sized, which is not the same as being big. There are no
handrails, no thresholds, no doorknobs, no sills, none of the small objects a
building uses to tell you how large it is. So it doesn't tell you. You will
stand at an edge and be unable to say whether the far side is thirty metres away
or three hundred, and the difference matters, and there is no way to find out
except to go.

Every other district is somebody's argument. The Unwitnessed is the null
hypothesis: uninterrupted growth, no observer, compounding for however long it
has been compounding. It is the largest region of the facility and the only one
with no name of its own — the surveyors marked it *unwitnessed* on the plans as
a status, meaning "come back and look at this", and nobody ever did, and the
status became the address.

It is also, quietly, the shape all the others are drifting toward.

> *What a team learns: nothing you know about scale applies. The route that
> looks like a shortcut is a day's climb, and the wall you thought was distant
> is a metre from your face.*

## Why one building has seven dialects

Because seven groups of people, at seven different times, worked out that the
walls did not stay put — and each of them did the most reasonable thing they
could think of.

Build something worth holding still for. Build nothing worth changing. Never
stop watching. Write it all down. Own almost nothing. Don't be here in the first
place.

And the seventh, which is not a strategy but a consequence: **stop looking**.

A player crossing from the Index into the Back is not changing level. They are
walking out of one civilisation's theory of permanence and into another's, and
the geometry is the argument. That is what all the chamfers and soffits and low
railings in the build plan are *for*. Not decoration. Somebody's position on the
question, built at full scale, in the only material available.
